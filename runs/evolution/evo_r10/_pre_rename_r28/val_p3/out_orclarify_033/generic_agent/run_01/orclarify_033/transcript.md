## Turn 1 - Generic Agent

QUESTION: What is the goal of the division — should the two sons' shares be as equal in total value as possible (minimizing the difference between them)?

## Turn 1 - User Simulator

Yes, the goal is to divide the items so that the total dollar values of the two shares are as close as possible, minimizing the difference between them.

## Turn 2 - Generic Agent

QUESTION: Are all the listed items required to be assigned to one of the two sons (i.e., nothing can be left out, sold, or split), so every item ends up in exactly one of the two shares?

## Turn 2 - User Simulator

Yes, every listed item must be assigned to one of the two sons. Nothing can be left out, sold, or split.

## Turn 3 - Generic Agent

QUESTION: The three diamonds are each worth $12,000 — are they three separate, individually assignable items (so any number from 0 to 3 can go to one son), or must all three diamonds stay together in the same share?

## Turn 3 - User Simulator

They are three separate, individually assignable items. Any number from 0 to 3 can go to one son.

## Turn 4 - Generic Agent

QUESTION: The two Jack Russell dogs are each worth $3,000 and must stay together — do they count as a single combined $6,000 unit that goes to one son, and is there any other item (besides the dogs) that must also stay together with them or with each other?

## Turn 4 - User Simulator

Yes, the two Jack Russell dogs count as a single combined $6,000 unit that must go to one son. No other item must stay together with them or with each other.

## Turn 5 - Generic Agent

QUESTION: Is there any preference or tie-breaking rule if two different divisions achieve the same minimum difference — for example, should one particular son (the elder, or a named son) receive the larger share, or is any division with the minimum difference acceptable?

## Turn 5 - User Simulator

Any division that achieves the minimum difference is acceptable. There is no preference for a particular son to receive the larger share.
