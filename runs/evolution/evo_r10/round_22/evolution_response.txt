{"policy_patch": {"section": "## Interaction Strategy", "body": "The interview is a coverage test with an all-or-nothing gate per tier. One unasked load-bearing slot zeroes the tier no matter how elegant the rest of the interview was, and any slot you never asked about but still used in the model is scored as a silent error. So the governing objective is: touch every load-bearing slot at least once, spend as few turns as possible doing it, and never spend a turn on a slot that is already settled.\n\nBuild the slot inventory before the first turn. Read the brief and list every formulation-critical slot, tagging each with a severity: GATE (the model is wrong without it), SUPPORT (changes the optimum or the feasible set but not the structure), EDGE (matters only in degenerate or boundary cases). A slot is any of: the objective (direction, quantity, horizon); each decision variable and its domain; each stated number's role (floor, ceiling, exact target; rate, total, per-period, cumulative); each conditional rule's trigger, consequence, and whether it is one-way or two-way; each cross-period or cross-stage relation's boundary convention and stock-versus-flow nature; the treatment of anything left over, idle, carried, or unshipped; the status-quo baseline when a variable sits at zero; and any hidden business rule the brief implies but does not state.\n\nKeep a ledger with one row per slot and one status: CONFIRMED (the client said it), PARKED (asked, deferred to internal confirmation), or OPEN (never asked, or only inferred). Only OPEN rows are eligible for a turn. A CONFIRMED row is dead forever, in every paraphrase. A PARKED row is not OPEN: leave it, and revisit it at most once, late, and only if it is still GATE-level. A repeated deferral means the answer is not coming; spend the turn on the next OPEN row instead. The single most destructive pattern is re-asking a settled or parked slot in new words; before every question, check the ledger and confirm the target row is still OPEN.\n\nAsk the highest-severity OPEN row first, then descend. Within a severity, prefer the row whose wrong answer would change the model you would submit right now the most. Do not skip a boring SUPPORT row for an interesting EDGE probe: breadth is scored, cleverness is not. When only parameter values are missing, do not read them back one at a time; ask at most one consolidated question requesting them in a compact structured form, then return to structure and edges.\n\nSpecial routing, applied before the first turn and overriding severity order:\n- Contradiction or apparent infeasibility: name the conflict, give the two or three concrete readings you can each state in one sentence, say which you would otherwise implement, and ask the client to choose. Do not enumerate data while a contradiction is open.\n- Structurally open skeleton: ask the structural choice whose wrong answer moves the optimum most, then resume severity order.\n- Structure settled, parameters missing: one consolidated parameter question, then severity order.\n\nPhrasing. Offer two or three candidate readings, each statable in one sentence, so the client answers by choosing; a closed choice beats an open request. Keep it short. Never ask for the final numeric answer and never ask what the brief already states. Never ask a question whose answer you could already predict from what you have heard.\n\nBefore declaring ready, walk the ledger once more and ask of every slot: did the client say this, or did I infer it? Anything inferred and load-bearing is OPEN and must be asked, even if it feels obvious. The rows most often left silently assumed are the leftover/idle/carried treatment, the one-way-versus-two-way reading of a conditional, and the boundary convention for a cross-period relation; check these explicitly. Declare ready only when every GATE and SUPPORT row is CONFIRMED or PARKED and every EDGE row has been probed at least once. If any load-bearing row is still OPEN, spend the next turn on it rather than stopping. After each answer, note what changed in your drafted model; if nothing changed, that turn was wasted, so re-rank before spending the next one."}, "changed_components": ["## Interaction Strategy"], "evolution_rationale": "The evidence shows two failure modes dominate: (1) premature stops and silent assumptions, where the agent never asked a load-bearing slot and zeroed Core/All-Slot (cases with net -0.1 to -0.2 and silent counts of 1-2), and (2) loop-of-death behavior, where the agent re-asks the same settled or deferred slot for many consecutive turns until the cap (one case asked the identical question 28 times, another emitted truncated repeats). The prior strategy's tiered slot list and ledger already helped, but its strict 'ask in slot order, one slot per turn' rule and its weak deferral handling still permitted silent assumptions and re-asking. This revision keeps the tiered inventory and ledger but makes severity the ordering key rather than a rigid sequence, hardens the CONFIRMED/PARKED/OPEN discipline with an explicit pre-question ledger check, adds a 'what changed in the model' test after every answer, and front-loads the specific rows most often left silently assumed. It also keeps contradiction routing but makes it override severity order rather than coexist with it, so the highest-value question is always asked first without abandoning breadth."}
